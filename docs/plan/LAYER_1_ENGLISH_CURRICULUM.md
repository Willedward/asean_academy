# Layer 1 English curriculum and learning design

**Prepared:** 18 October 2026  
**Status:** proposed v1 curriculum for educator review  
**Audience:** secondary-school students in Indonesia and Southeast Asia preparing for
the ASEAN Scholarship selection process  
**Depends on:** [LAYER_1_ENGLISH_SCOPE.md](./LAYER_1_ENGLISH_SCOPE.md)

## 1. Purpose and evidence boundary

This curriculum converts the current candidate-reported test format into teachable,
measurable skills. It is a preparation curriculum, not a claim to reproduce a confidential
or officially published MOE syllabus.

The current working test blueprint is:

- **Section 1 — Language use and comprehension:** 90 minutes;
- **Part 1:** vocabulary in context, presented as sentence-level multiple choice;
- **Part 2:** a passage with blanks and a surplus word bank;
- **Part 3:** an open cloze passage without a word bank;
- **Part 4:** two reading passages with vocabulary-in-context, reference and
  comprehension questions; and
- **Section 2 — Writing:** 30 minutes, with a candidate report indicating a response of
  about 300 words.

The order, durations and task descriptions are supported by a recent candidate account and
are consistent with independent preparation summaries. The public MOE applicant guide
describes selection-test administration but does not publish the detailed paper blueprint.
Exact question counts, marks, number of essay choices and word-limit wording therefore
remain configurable until stronger evidence is available. Every source record should store
the test year, entry level, country, city and whether the information is official,
candidate-reported or inferred.

Use reports and screenshots to identify constructs and difficulty. Do not copy a live,
recalled or copyrighted question into the learner bank. All NextScholar passages, prompts,
questions and explanations must be original.

## 2. Curriculum model

English should not copy the Mathematics hierarchy of isolated content chapters. Language
skills accumulate and interact: vocabulary supports cloze and reading, reading provides
models for writing, and writing exposes grammar weaknesses. The course therefore uses a
spiral structure with three stages.

| Stage | Purpose | Modules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Build the language toolkit | Learn reusable vocabulary, grammar and sentence-analysis strategies | E0–E2 |
| B — Apply it to the paper | Apply those tools to banked cloze, open cloze and reading | E3–E5 |
| C — Compose and perform | Plan, draft, revise and complete both papers under time pressure | E6–E9 |

Every later module retrieves earlier vocabulary and grammar. Learners do not “finish
vocabulary” before reading or writing; they meet high-value word families repeatedly in
sentences, passages and their own compositions.

### Proposed Beta size

- 10 modules;
- 51 core lessons of approximately 25–35 minutes;
- 9 module checkpoints;
- 4 cumulative milestone assessments;
- 2 full Section 1 forms and a protected reserve form;
- a prompt bank for guided, untimed and timed writing; and
- 2 full simulated English papers before Beta completion.

Fifty-one lessons is the content target, not a compulsory lockstep journey. E0 assigns a
learner one of three starting paths using deterministic outcome scores:

- **Foundation path:** E1 and E2 before exam-format modules;
- **Standard path:** the complete E1–E9 sequence; or
- **Accelerated path:** checkpoints can validate E1/E2 competence, but cumulative review
  continues to retrieve their skills.

This is prerequisite routing, not the Layer 2 personalised study planner.

### Recurring strands between lessons

The modules provide explicit instruction, but four short strands continue throughout the
course so that knowledge is retained and transferred:

1. **Vocabulary retrieval:** 5–10 minutes using due word profiles in new sentence and
   passage contexts, including both recognition and production.
2. **Wide reading:** at least one short narrative and one informational text each week,
   followed by a brief summary, vocabulary inference and one discussion or reflection
   question. Texts must be original, openly licensed or properly licensed.
3. **Sentence craft:** one weekly combine, expand or repair task based on common learner
   errors.
4. **Writing fluency:** one five-minute plan or ten-minute paragraph each week, even while
   the learner is studying the objective paper.

A sustainable schedule is four or five 30–45 minute sessions per week: two new lessons,
one application lesson, one retrieval/retry session and, when available, one timed or
writing session. The complete sequence will normally take longer than a short cramming
course; a diagnostic can reduce unnecessary foundation lessons without removing
cumulative review.

## 3. Standard lesson anatomy

Each lesson follows a consistent learn–apply–retrieve cycle.

1. **Retrieval warm-up (3–5 minutes):** two to four previously taught words, grammar
   decisions or reading inferences.
2. **Explicit model (5–7 minutes):** explain one strategy and demonstrate the reasoning
   aloud on an original example.
3. **Worked analysis (5–7 minutes):** annotate why the correct response fits and why a
   plausible distractor fails.
4. **Guided practice (5–8 minutes):** scaffolded items with prompts that gradually fade.
5. **Independent exam application (8–12 minutes):** unseen items in the target format.
6. **Error classification (2–3 minutes):** identify the cause, such as unknown meaning,
   missed contrast, wrong word class, weak reference tracking or unsupported inference.
7. **Exit ticket:** one unseen transfer item that determines whether the lesson is
   complete or enters the retry queue.

Writing lessons use the same structure as a **Model–Practice–Reflect** cycle: study a
model, practise one controllable move, apply it independently, then compare the result
against a criterion. This follows the evidence-based approach in the US Institute of
Education Sciences practice guide *Teaching Secondary Students to Write Effectively*.

Lesson explanations and answer rationales are authored and reviewed in advance. Objective
practice does not require a live AI call.

## 4. Modules and lessons

### E0 — Orientation, baseline and error language

**Purpose:** establish a trustworthy starting point and teach the learner how to use
feedback.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E0.1 Know the paper | Identify the purpose, format and timing of every reported test part without treating provisional counts as official facts. |
| E0.2 Baseline objective check | Complete an untutored, mixed diagnostic covering vocabulary, grammar/cloze and reading. |
| E0.3 Diagnose the mistake | Classify errors by knowledge, reasoning, attention and time rather than recording only right or wrong. |

The baseline reports skill priorities. It does not estimate an official scholarship-test
score and it excludes essay scoring.

### E1 — Vocabulary that transfers

**Purpose:** build word knowledge while teaching learners how to reason about unfamiliar
words. This module directly supports Part 1 and supplies material for Parts 2–4 and writing.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E1.1 Meaning from local context | Use definitions, examples, contrast, cause and tone around a word to test a possible meaning. |
| E1.2 Word class and word family | Use noun, verb, adjective and adverb forms to eliminate grammatically impossible options. |
| E1.3 Roots and affixes | Use common prefixes, roots and suffixes as evidence without assuming they always determine the whole meaning. |
| E1.4 Synonym precision | Distinguish denotation, strength and connotation among near-synonyms. |
| E1.5 Collocation and register | Select words that naturally combine and fit formal, neutral or informal contexts. |
| E1.6 Unknown-word protocol | Combine syntax, context, morphology and option elimination under a short time limit. |

#### Vocabulary teaching policy

Vocabulary is not a random list and the course should not promise that a fixed list will
contain every tested word. Each selected word receives a reusable **word profile**:

- learner-friendly meaning;
- pronunciation support where needed;
- part of speech and useful word-family forms;
- two common collocations;
- synonym, contrast and connotation notes;
- one clear example and one non-example; and
- at least one later appearance in a cloze, reading passage or writing model.

The first Beta should prioritise broadly useful academic and descriptive word families,
connectives and high-value collocations. Avoid rare words chosen only to look difficult.
Teach approximately 8–10 target word families in one introduction set, then retrieve them
across expanding intervals and varied contexts. Reviews require both recognition and
production: choose the meaning, complete a new sentence, distinguish a near-synonym and
use the word appropriately in a short response.

The US Institute of Education Sciences adolescent-literacy guide recommends explicit
vocabulary instruction, repeated exposure in multiple contexts, meaningful use and
strategies for learning unfamiliar words independently. Research on second-language
vocabulary also supports spacing retrieval instead of relying on one concentrated study
session.

**E1 checkpoint:** infer unfamiliar meanings, distinguish near-synonyms and apply word
families/collocations in unseen sentences. A configurable initial mastery target is 80%
untimed followed by 75% on a short timed mixed set.

### E2 — Sentence control and cohesion

**Purpose:** make the grammar needed for Parts 2 and 3 visible and usable. Teach grammar as
meaning and sentence control rather than terminology memorisation.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E2.1 Sentence and clause boundaries | Recognise complete clauses, fragments, run-ons and relationships between clauses. |
| E2.2 Agreement and reference | Control subject–verb agreement, pronoun agreement and clear antecedents. |
| E2.3 Time and viewpoint | Select tense and aspect from the passage timeline and maintain consistency. |
| E2.4 Noun phrases | Use articles, determiners, quantity and countability accurately. |
| E2.5 Prepositions and patterns | Choose common preposition, verb-complement and adjective-complement patterns in context. |
| E2.6 Logical connections | Use conjunctions, transitions and relative structures to express contrast, cause, condition, addition and sequence. |

Grammar practice moves from one controlled contrast to mixed passage decisions. Every
explanation answers both “what form fits?” and “what meaning or relationship does it
express?”

**E2 checkpoint:** complete a mixed editing and gap set, justify the evidence for each
choice and correct recurring error categories.

### E3 — Banked story cloze

**Purpose:** prepare for a passage containing more candidate words than blanks.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E3.1 Read the whole story first | Track setting, characters, chronology, conflict and tone before filling gaps. |
| E3.2 Apply the grammar filter | Use the words around a blank to identify the required word class and form. |
| E3.3 Apply the meaning filter | Compare collocation, connotation and local meaning among grammatically possible words. |
| E3.4 Solve globally | Reconsider earlier choices as the remaining bank and whole-passage coherence change the evidence. |

The learner interface treats one passage as one assessment object. Words are selectable
without requiring drag-and-drop, used words remain visible, and learners can clear or
change answers. Practice feedback explains local syntax and global narrative evidence.

**E3 checkpoint:** complete two unseen passages, including one timed passage, without
receiving per-gap feedback until the passage is submitted.

### E4 — Open cloze without a word bank

**Purpose:** construct the missing word from grammatical and discourse evidence instead of
recognising it from supplied options.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E4.1 Function-word gaps | Supply articles, pronouns, auxiliaries, prepositions and conjunctions from sentence structure. |
| E4.2 Form and agreement gaps | Infer the required tense, number, comparison or derived word form. |
| E4.3 Collocation and lexical gaps | Retrieve a content word or fixed expression that fits the passage meaning. |
| E4.4 Cohesion across sentences | Use reference chains, repetition, contrast and chronology beyond the immediate sentence. |
| E4.5 Timed open cloze | Apply a first-pass, second-pass and final-read method to a complete passage. |

Every authored gap must have one defensible canonical answer or an explicit accepted-answer
set. Reviewers must try to produce unintended valid alternatives. Broad semantic matching
or live AI judging is not permitted for objective scores.

**E4 checkpoint:** complete one mixed open-cloze passage under time and explain the clue
type used for selected answers.

### E5 — Reading comprehension and reference

**Purpose:** prepare for two passages and questions about meaning, reference, detail and
inference.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E5.1 Build a passage map | Identify topic, paragraph purpose, development and text structure without over-annotating. |
| E5.2 Track reference chains | Resolve what pronouns, demonstratives and descriptive phrases refer to. |
| E5.3 Meaning and synonym in context | Replace a word with a meaning that preserves its sense and grammatical role in that sentence. |
| E5.4 Retrieve stated evidence | Locate and paraphrase explicit information accurately. |
| E5.5 Make bounded inferences | Combine textual clues without importing unsupported assumptions. |
| E5.6 Purpose, tone and main idea | Distinguish the writer’s central purpose, attitude and supporting details. |
| E5.7 Two-passage pacing | Allocate time, mark uncertain items and preserve a checking buffer across both passages. |

The reading routine is **Locate → Link → Prove**:

1. locate the relevant sentence or paragraph;
2. link it to the question and surrounding context; and
3. prove the response with a specific textual clue.

**E5 checkpoint:** answer an unseen two-passage set under a configurable time limit and
attach evidence locations during review. Evidence locations teach reasoning but are not
required in the real-test simulation.

### E6 — From prompt to organised response

**Purpose:** remove the blank-page problem before expecting polished language.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E6.1 Decode the task | Identify topic, command, audience, scope and any required personal application. |
| E6.2 Generate usable ideas | Produce relevant examples rapidly using experience, observation, school learning and plausible scenarios. |
| E6.3 Choose and order | Select two or three developable ideas instead of listing many shallow points. |
| E6.4 Build the controlling focus | Write a clear central response that every paragraph advances. |
| E6.5 Plan in five minutes | Produce a compact introduction–body–conclusion plan with examples and transitions. |

**E6 milestone:** create a relevant, coherent plan for three unseen prompts within five
minutes each. At least one prompt should resemble the reported reflective application of
school learning to life beyond school.

### E7 — Paragraphs, evidence and cohesion

**Purpose:** turn a plan into developed writing rather than a sequence of assertions.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E7.1 Paragraph purpose | Give each paragraph one controlling point or narrative function. |
| E7.2 Develop with specifics | Explain significance and use precise examples, actions, observations or consequences. |
| E7.3 Cohesion without formula | Connect ideas through reference, logical sequence and selective transitions rather than repetitive stock phrases. |
| E7.4 Introductions and endings | Open directly, establish direction and conclude by resolving the response rather than repeating it mechanically. |
| E7.5 Read like a writer | Analyse an original model and transfer one structural move into a new paragraph. |

**E7 checkpoint:** revise an underdeveloped response and independently write a coherent
multi-paragraph response from a supplied plan.

### E8 — Accurate and effective essay language

**Purpose:** improve clarity and control while preventing forced “advanced vocabulary.”

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E8.1 Precise vocabulary | Replace vague or repetitive words with accurate choices that fit the meaning and register. |
| E8.2 Sentence variety with control | Combine and vary sentences without introducing fragments, agreement errors or unclear reference. |
| E8.3 Show relationships | Express cause, contrast, qualification, sequence and consequence explicitly. |
| E8.4 Edit by priority | Check task fulfilment and organisation before grammar, spelling and punctuation. |
| E8.5 Personal error log | Identify two recurring error patterns and apply a short correction routine. |

The curriculum should never reward an uncommon word merely for being uncommon. A simple,
precise word earns more instructional credit than a sophisticated word used incorrectly.

**E8 checkpoint:** improve a flawed draft, explain each major revision and demonstrate the
same language control on a new paragraph.

### E9 — Essay forms and 30-minute performance

**Purpose:** transfer the writing process across likely prompt families and then compress
it to the reported test time.

| Lesson | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| E9.1 Reflective and personal expository writing | Connect experience or school learning to a clear insight, explanation or application. |
| E9.2 Narrative writing | Shape setting, conflict, purposeful detail, turning point and resolution where a narrative prompt is offered. |
| E9.3 Opinion and discursive writing | State a defensible position, develop reasons and acknowledge relevant complexity. |
| E9.4 Compress the process | Move from 45-minute supported writing to 35-minute and then 30-minute independent writing. |
| E9.5 Full English simulation | Complete Section 1 and Section 2 under their separate reported limits, with no hints or immediate marking. |

Prompt families remain provisional until the source inventory confirms what is used for
the relevant entry level and year. Do not assume that every sitting offers every family.

## 5. Essay instruction and feedback system

### Internal formative rubric

Because no official public scoring rubric has been established, NextScholar should use a
versioned internal rubric and label all feedback as preparation guidance. Use five
criteria, each with four behaviourally described bands:

1. **Task fulfilment and ideas:** relevance, focus, development and meaningful detail;
2. **Organisation and cohesion:** purposeful sequencing, paragraph unity and connections;
3. **Vocabulary and register:** precision, range, collocation and suitability;
4. **Sentence control and grammar:** clarity, variety and accuracy; and
5. **Mechanics:** spelling, punctuation and paragraph presentation.

Do not publish a fabricated probability of winning the scholarship or convert the rubric
into an “official ASEAN score.” The overall indicator is less important than criterion
evidence and the learner’s next action.

### Feedback sequence

Feedback should be limited enough to act on:

- two evidence-linked strengths;
- one primary content/organisation priority;
- one primary language priority;
- selected corrections that illustrate a repeated pattern rather than correcting every
  line;
- one short revision task; and
- one transfer goal for the next unseen prompt.

After feedback, the learner revises one paragraph or planning decision before attempting a
new prompt. Repeatedly resubmitting the same essay merely to increase an AI estimate should
not count as independent improvement.

### Writing milestones

| Milestone | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| W1 — Relevant | The response addresses every part of the prompt and avoids a memorised off-topic essay. |
| W2 — Planned | The learner creates a usable plan within five minutes. |
| W3 — Developed | Body paragraphs contain specific development rather than lists of claims. |
| W4 — Controlled | Meaning remains clear despite ambitious sentence and vocabulary choices. |
| W5 — Timed | The learner plans, drafts approximately the required length and checks within 30 minutes. |
| W6 — Transferable | Improvements appear on a new prompt without copying feedback wording. |

An educator-reviewed anchor set should include weak, developing, secure and strong original
responses for several prompt families. AI feedback remains in administrator evaluation
until it shows acceptable agreement, evidence grounding, consistency, age-appropriate tone
and cost on that set.

## 6. Practice, checkpoints and mocks

### Modes

| Mode | Hints | Feedback timing | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learn | Strategy prompts and explanations | During the activity | Acquire one method |
| Guided practice | Fading prompts | After each response or passage | Stabilise the method |
| Checkpoint | None | After submission | Verify module mastery |
| Timed practice | None | After the complete part | Build pacing and concentration |
| Full mock | None | After both papers are locked | Rehearse performance conditions |

Passage tasks should not reveal one answer while other blanks or questions remain active;
that can leak information into later responses. The system scores each blank or question
for analytics but preserves passage-level presentation and timing.

### Cumulative milestones

1. **M1 — Language toolkit:** E1/E2 mixed checkpoint.
2. **M2 — Cloze control:** one banked and one open cloze completed back-to-back.
3. **M3 — Reading control:** two passages completed with stable accuracy and pacing.
4. **M4 — Exam readiness:** two separate full simulations, using no practice or diagnostic
   item in either form.

Initial internal mastery thresholds may use 80% untimed and 75% timed, but they are product
defaults rather than claims about scholarship cut-offs. Beta response data should calibrate
difficulty and thresholds without rewriting historical attempts.

## 7. Content-authoring blueprint

Every learner-visible item is tagged with one primary outcome and, where useful, secondary
skills.

| Prefix | Domain | Example outcomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `EV` | Vocabulary | context clue, synonym precision, word family, collocation, register |
| `EG` | Grammar/cohesion | clause, agreement, tense, determiner, preposition, connector |
| `EB` | Banked cloze | grammar filter, meaning filter, global coherence |
| `EO` | Open cloze | function word, form, lexical choice, cross-sentence cohesion |
| `ER` | Reading | reference, vocabulary in context, detail, inference, purpose/tone |
| `EW` | Writing | prompt, plan, development, cohesion, vocabulary, grammar, editing |
| `EX` | Integrated exam | pacing, part completion, full-paper simulation |

Content pools remain separate:

- lesson examples;
- guided practice;
- independent practice;
- retry review;
- module checkpoints;
- baseline diagnostic;
- endline diagnostic;
- full mocks; and
- protected reserve items.

The English review gate must check language accuracy, one-best-answer determinism,
unintended accepted answers, cultural accessibility across Southeast Asia, passage
coherence, distractor plausibility, original authorship and alignment to the declared
outcome.

## 8. What the learner should be able to do at completion

An English-ready learner can:

- infer and test unfamiliar vocabulary instead of depending on memorised definitions;
- retrieve a growing bank of useful word families and collocations across varied contexts;
- use syntax and discourse clues to solve banked and open cloze passages;
- trace references, locate evidence and distinguish stated meaning from inference;
- manage two reading passages without sacrificing a checking buffer;
- interpret an unseen writing prompt and produce a focused plan within five minutes;
- develop and organise a response of approximately the required length within 30 minutes;
- revise high-impact content and language weaknesses using an evidence-linked rubric; and
- explain recurring errors and apply the correction on a new task.

## 9. Curriculum approval and implementation order

Before high-volume authoring:

1. obtain English-educator review of the outcomes, terminology and formative rubric;
2. add metadata from the recent candidate source and any additional reports;
3. confirm or leave configurable the exact counts, marks, prompt choices and word rule;
4. freeze v1 stable outcome keys and question-type contracts; and
5. author a pilot vertical slice: E1.1, E3.1 and E6.1 with their practice and checkpoint
   evidence.

The pilot deliberately covers vocabulary, passage interaction and writing instruction. It
can validate lesson depth and UI requirements before committing to all 51 lessons.
