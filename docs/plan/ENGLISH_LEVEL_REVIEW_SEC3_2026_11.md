# English level review for Secondary 3 entry

**Date:** 4 November 2026
**Target:** Indonesian and Southeast Asian applicants for Secondary 3 entry, about 14–15, learning
English as a second language and tested at Secondary 1 and 2 level.
**Replaces:** the level judgements in [ENGLISH_CONTENT_AUDIT_2026_11.md](./ENGLISH_CONTENT_AUDIT_2026_11.md)
§4.6 and §5.3, which assumed a Secondary 1-entry band of about 11–12. Findings on keys, leakage,
cues and format are unchanged.

## Status and limits

This is an advisory pre-review reading and records no approval. There is no official test
specification and no corpus tool was used, so CEFR and reading-grade figures are estimates. The
ranges below are proposals for the English educator to accept or change. The real paper's
difficulty is unknown, and a real paper or educator review is most likely to move rules 1, 5 and 7.

The review covered all 35 `ev-*` items, word counts for all 37 passages (14 read), measurements of
all 43 lessons (8 read closely), the rubric and the essay prompt.

## Verdict by strand

| Strand | Verdict | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vocabulary | Words hard, items easy | Targets are mostly B2–C1, some C2 (*sporadic*, *obsolete*). Context and weak distractors make many solvable at about B1. Some are A2–B1 but labelled difficulty 3 (`ev-l5-012`, `ev-l5-010`). |
| Grammar | Too easy and narrow | Mostly A2–B1 with explicit cues (`eg-l3-004`: "Every Friday" → *sorts*). No `eg-*` item names passives, conditionals, reported speech, modals or relative clauses. |
| Cloze | Too easy in diagnostics, about right in practice | Baseline open-cloze keys are *a, they, that, but, after* at labelled difficulty 4 (`eo-l5-003`). Passages are short. `eo-l3-001` and `eo-l2-001` are well pitched. |
| Reading | Language about right, diagnostics too short | Passages are about B1+–B2. Exit and checkpoint texts run 296–438 words; baseline is 162; endline and reserve are 129. |
| Writing | Demands about right, wording too hard | The prompt is plain. Focus, concession and qualified claims suit the band; the question stems do not. |
| Teaching language | E1–E5 acceptable, E0 and E6–E9 too hard | Estimated reading grades: E1–E5 8.1–10.4, E6–E9 mostly 9.3–13.2, E0 about 13. Lessons are often harder than the passages they teach. |
| Rubric | Standard about right, wording educator-facing | Keep the standard. Add plainer wording if learners see it. |

**Main new finding: the baseline is probably too easy.** Its four parts are effectively B1, so strong
applicants may hit the ceiling and have little room to show growth. This is unmeasured and needs a
small pilot.

## Authoring rules for new and revised English

1. **Vocabulary mix:** tested targets about 60–70% B2, 20–30% C1, at most 10% C2 and one C2 per set.
   Use A2–B1 only in guided practice, and never label it difficulty 3 or above.
2. **Difficulty comes from distractors, not rare words.** Use near-synonym or same-class distractors,
   as in `ev-l4-003` (*assertive, aggressive, arrogant*). Outside guided practice, don't state the
   definition in the sentence (`ev-l1-001`).
3. **Glosses:** at or below the target's level, never one rarer synonym alone.
4. **Teaching prose:** estimated reading grade 9–10 or lower, sentences averaging about 16 words or
   fewer, at most one new term per section with a gloss. Prefer concrete examples.
5. **Passage length:** reading 300–450 words per passage, including baseline and endline. Cloze
   150–250 words with 8–10 gaps. The paper reports no lengths, so these follow the existing exit
   tickets.
6. **Cloze mix:** every open-cloze diagnostic includes lexical or collocation gaps at B1–B2, not only
   function words. Banks include same-class surplus words.
7. **Grammar range:** add passives, conditionals, reported speech and relative clauses at B1–B2. An
   adverbial cue must not solve the item alone.
8. **Grammar terms:**
   - Use freely: noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, article, preposition, conjunction, subject,
     object, clause, tense names. The educator should confirm Indonesian students know these.
   - Gloss first: independent and dependent clause, fragment, run-on, comma splice, relative clause,
     determiner, collocation, concession.
   - Replace: *finite verb* ("verb showing tense"), *head subject* ("main noun of the subject"),
     *antecedent* ("word the pronoun refers to"). Avoid *distributive*.
9. **Banned from learner text:** construct, blueprint, confound, norm, process compression, fade
   support, given-to-new, synonym drift, reverse outline, reflective hinge, conceptual closure,
   structural abstraction, dominant function, operationalise.
10. **Routines:** teach as plain questions ("What must I answer? Who reads it?"), not T-C-A-S-P, I-S-A-R
    or R-D-L. At most one per module, and never test a routine's name.
11. **Rubric:** gloss *proportionate, economical, qualification*, and keep the standards.
12. **Pilot the baseline:** flag parts answered correctly by more than about 85% of learners, and
    revise difficulty labels from data.
